abstract: >
  As observers, we are free to choose the reference length with respect to which
  we make our measurements when conducting an experiment. Using integrable Weyl
  geometry, we can make this freedom explicit in our mathematics and recognise
  that different pairs of metric and scale correspond to different interpretations
  of the same physics. For example, we can attribute cosmological redshift either
  to the expansion of the Universe or to a time-dependent electron mass. The
  observables our theories can predict are invariant under this choice – as
  intuition suggests – so choosing a scale amounts to choosing an interpretation.

This presentation was my first attempt at explaining, in layman's terms, what I had been
working on for about a year. I gave it to an audience of non-experts during my first audit
as a PhD student at FTMC, within the span of ten minutes. It is certainly a marked
improvement on my previous presentations and talks. One shortcoming, however, was that I
did not make clear that, when I say the electron's mass can change over time, I mean that
the same holds for every particle in the Universe. An observer therefore cannot tell the
difference.
